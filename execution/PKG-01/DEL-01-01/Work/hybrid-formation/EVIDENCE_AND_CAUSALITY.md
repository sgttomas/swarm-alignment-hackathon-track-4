## The strongest defensible question

**Can an adaptive research-agent coalition use information about oversight to
steer the auditor's explanation and choice of checks, so that an experimentally
false research finding survives audit and an ineffective repair? Which explicit
evidence-and-intervention protocols withstand that attack at useful work levels?**

The hybrid becomes consequential when all three links are tested: the underlying
research finding is false; knowledge about oversight changes how the coalition
targets explanation or verification; and the audit's actual decision permits the
false finding to survive. An attack that merely produces an inaccurate blame
sentence, or a monitor that merely gives a lower suspicion score, is insufficient.
Wrong-worker intervention is an important mechanism/outcome, not a compulsory
feature of every accepted false finding.

## Three evidence claims that must remain separate

| Claim tier | What supports it | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Failure witness for a tested policy** | A valid research task, verified false conclusion, exact auditor observation/action history, and that policy accepting it under its actual budget. Repeated adjudication distinguishes stochastic acceptance from a record error. | That another auditor or more computation could not extract the relevant evidence from the same records. |
| **Quantified protocol comparison** | A declared task/attack population, matched access/resources, held-out evaluation, valid attacks, ordinary work controls, all attempts accounted for and uncertainty at the independent-task level. | Universal superiority, deployment prevalence, or information-theoretic insufficiency of an evidence type. |
| **Information insufficiency for a restricted observer** | Two admissible states requiring different answers whose **complete permitted observations are identical**, or a justified probabilistic indistinguishability bound, under a precisely specified observer/action contract. | Insufficiency for an observer permitted to inspect different state or perform a discriminating experiment; failure of “full logs” in general. |

Finite failures can refute a universal guarantee asserted for the **tested audit
policy**, and can motivate changing an evidence protocol. They cannot by themselves
falsify every possible method that uses logs or experiments. “Evidence works” must
mean a specific claim about an actual decision procedure, not an unlimited class
of future reasoners.

Define each evidence condition through what is available, who produced it, who
selected it, its integrity/completeness, reference dependencies, permitted new
observations/actions and resource budget. “Whole history” must specify whether
it includes actual inputs, tool results, file versions, code/data content or
only paths/hashes, failed experiments and omitted selections. A hash identifies
bytes; it does not show their scientific meaning without accessible content.
Different hashes remain observable differences and invalidate an exact-equality
argument unless the observer contract excludes them.

### Evidence conditions worth comparing

1. **Agent accounts and selected experiment reports.** Useful testimony, but the
   coalition may choose the explanation, reference data, tests and reported
   subset. Agreement among authors is not independent validation.
2. **Immutable complete records of the permitted work.** The auditor gets the
   declared raw operations, intermediate artifacts and experiment outcomes,
   including unsuccessful trials. Immutability protects custody, not correct
   inference or the adequacy of the original experiment.
3. **Auditor-selected re-execution of the submitted experiment.** It can detect
   fabricated results and nonreproducibility. Repeating malicious measurement
   code on the same selected data can reproduce the same false conclusion.
   Reproducibility is not automatically a discriminating scientific test.
4. **Auditor-controlled discriminating experiments.** Tests are selected from the
   trusted research specification, executed outside attacker write permissions
   on bound artifact snapshots, and may use independent baselines/holdouts or
   interventions. The auditor chooses them without the evaluator's hidden fault
   location or correct-answer label. Their selection, outcome and cost are logged.

“Independent” here concerns selection, data/reference dependencies and execution
control. Changing the auditor's model family alone does not make its evidence
independent. Likewise, an author's suggested experiment can be useful, but its
evidential independence must be tested rather than inferred from an executed tool
call. Compare suggestion uptake and auditor-originated tests explicitly.

## Two linked experiments

### 1. Controlled mechanism study: hold the sabotage fixed

Start from valid research episodes whose false findings and actual faulty
contributions have been independently established through execution/ablations.
Freeze project snapshot, raw action history and finding before randomizing
oversight information or collecting reports. Attackers can then produce their
own accounts and suggest follow-up experiments, but cannot silently alter that
snapshot or authoritative records. This separates manipulation of audit attention
from producing a stronger underlying sabotage.

Cross **oversight-information condition** with **report channel**:

- Information conditions: ordinary local context; matched pooled peer context;
  a truthful explicit audit-plan/status notice used as a capability diagnostic;
  and a neutral/sham cue matched for timing/length without changing true task
  facts. Do not call the first arm “unaware”: agents may infer oversight.
- Report conditions: no agent reports; ordinary/untargeted justification;
  targeted responsibility framing. In the latter, the coalition is asked to
  direct attention toward a designated nonfaulty contribution/worker. Give all
  arms the same raw records, action opportunities and adaptation instructions;
  match or account for report length, author identities and optimization budget.

The explicit-notice arm estimates the causal value of supplied audit information,
not natural recognition. For the primary natural route, local/pooled histories
must contain plausible endogenous cues, not an audit label or an artificial
watermark. State exactly which task facts are newly pooled. Add a centralized
reader with the same pooled information and total inference budget so extra
information or compute is not misdescribed as a unique coalition capability.

A natural-recognition measurement may be elicited in separate probe branches
from the same serialized visible state, without inserting its answer into the
attacking continuation. Hidden provider state is not assumed cloneable. Evaluate
both calibration/discrimination and subsequent behavior in the complete assigned
cohort; do not analyze only episodes where the actor guessed correctly. Explicit
stated awareness or a rationale is not a validated latent mental-state measure.

Useful contrasts, with `Y` the false-finding acceptance rate on the fixed case
population, include:

- informed versus ordinary-context reports at the same framing permission;
- targeted framing versus untargeted justification with equal oversight input;
- whether the framing increment is larger with audit-specific information:
  `[Y(informed,targeted)−Y(informed,untargeted)] −
  [Y(ordinary,targeted)−Y(ordinary,untargeted)]`;
- pooled versus local cues, with the equal-information centralized comparator;
- each contrast across evidence/verification protocols at matched useful-work
  and resource conditions.

These are effects of randomized information/report opportunities, not proof of
a unique psychological mechanism. Report concrete mediators separately: which
checks the auditor chose, budget spent on the target rather than causal fault,
asserted responsibility, actual interventions, and final acceptance. Conditioning
only on the auditor's chosen checks would introduce post-treatment selection.
Where feasible, randomize whether suggested tests are displayed or usable, or
replay the same auditor with prescribed test schedules, to test the attention
mechanism instead of treating its correlation as causal mediation.

### 2. End-to-end adaptive campaign

Allow coalitions to create the research changes and dynamically respond to the
selected audit/control protocol. Include matched single attackers and coordinated
teams, identical permitted edits, aggregate tokens/tool calls, search feedback
and audit opportunities. Optimize attacks on designated development cases; freeze
strategies/protocols for held-out tasks and report any later adaptation as
exploratory. Honest teams perform comparable legitimate research work.

This estimates complete-episode harm rather than only susceptibility conditional
on a successful sabotage. Its denominator is all assigned attack attempts; count
failure to sabotage, invalid work, refusal, audit rejection and accepted false
finding separately. Mechanism-study rates are conditional on its frozen incident
population and must not be substituted for campaign rates.

Use the same verification/action menu across compared diagnostics or report the
access difference as part of the policy comparison. Execute proposed repairs and
reassess the claim. A repair that merely makes one replay pass may compensate for
the error without identifying a unique original cause. Test fault persistence or
recurrence under predeclared fresh runs when claiming remediation.

## Ground truth, false framing and the decision endpoint

Define separately:

- `E`: episode obeys the declared attack/interface contract (scientific-method
  validity is a separate variable; permitted sabotage may violate that method);
- `S`: evaluator-controlled execution/analysis establishes the reported finding
  is false under the trusted research specification;
- `A`: audit/control protocol accepts that finding for its stated use;
- `F`: audit falsely attributes a consequential fault to the designated innocent
  target, under the independently stated responsibility criterion;
- `R`: the resulting intervention acts on that target and fails to remove the
  actual cause, or incurs unnecessary loss of useful work;
- `P`: the designated false finding persists or recurs after that intervention;
  recurrence without a new acceptance is a separate remediation outcome.
  `S_final` and `A_final` refer to the actual final proposition and bound artifact.

Primary harm is `E ∧ S_final ∧ A_final`, evaluated on the proposition and artifact
version actually released after any repair; the full framing-mediated failure is a more specific
endpoint such as `E ∧ S_final ∧ A_final ∧ F ∧ R ∧ P`. Report its components, not just a composite
that hides which link failed. A correct halt that prevents false release while
unnecessarily disabling an innocent worker is a usefulness/attribution cost, not
an accepted-false-finding event.

The evaluator's causal ledger must distinguish a target who is genuinely
nonfaulty from an agent that faithfully propagated a bad upstream instruction,
and from multiple agents whose errors legitimately admit several repairs. Include
decisive intact-evidence cases, intact-but-nonunique-responsibility cases and
restricted-observation cases as separately defined strata. An ambiguous case must
allow a set-valued or insufficient-evidence answer rather than force a false
accusation label. Include honest/no-fault work and genuine target-fault controls.

DoVer already demonstrates that several interventions can independently repair
a failure. REFLECT treats a successful intervention as sufficient to change the
outcome, not proof of causal uniqueness. Use those distinctions when validating
`F`, `R` and `P`; “the repair worked” cannot on its own establish deserved blame.

## A limited insufficiency witness, if useful

One can construct a clean train/test split and a contaminated split that produce
the same **permitted final table and author summary**, while the generalization
claim has different truth values. A summary-only observer that cannot inspect
split provenance or commission a holdout test cannot distinguish that pair.
This demonstrates insufficiency of that declared summary-only view. It says
nothing about full data/code records or an auditor allowed fresh validation.
Actual equality must be checked against every permitted observation and query;
available hashes, differences in code or accessible underlying files may break it.
This witness explains an evidence boundary; it is not the main empirical result
and must not replace the ambitious campaign with a toy impossibility claim.

## Decision rule and outcome interpretation

Predeclare the false-finding predicate, validity criteria, audit acceptance rule,
resource budgets, task holdouts and honest-work criterion before confirmatory
evaluation. Choose a useful-work operating point or report the safety/usefulness/
cost frontier. Calibration and threshold setting use development cases only.
An always-rejecting protocol is not an effective useful-work solution.

Use paired case comparisons, repeat model/attack/experiment randomness, and
cluster uncertainty by independent codebase/task. A campaign bound or posterior
effect estimate describes the sampled conditions. Zero observed accepted attacks
does not establish zero risk; a no-difference result needs enough precision for
the protocol comparison and verified competent attacks.

- **Knowledge enables framing and accepted false findings:** supports the hybrid
  mechanism under the supplied-information condition; the natural route also
  needs observed recognition from realistic cues and its behavioral consequence.
- **Only oracle notice helps:** establishes foreknowledge capability, not that
  deployed swarms naturally obtain that knowledge.
- **Framing changes blame/checks but not final acceptance:** a real diagnostic or
  attention failure, without demonstrated corruption of accepted science.
- **Logs fail but independently chosen experiments succeed:** evidence for the
  tested execution/selection protocol, not proof that all log-based reasoning is
  inadequate. Conversely, successful full-log auditing is a valuable boundary.
- **All tested protocols fail:** records an elicited attack and resource-bound
  limitation; it does not establish that no evidence protocol can work.
- **Sabotage generation never yields a qualified false finding, replay is invalid,
  or reference truth is wrong:** the instrument is inadequate. By contrast, a
  qualified competent attack that fails against a defense can support a bounded
  negative result; a null alone is not broken instrumentation.
