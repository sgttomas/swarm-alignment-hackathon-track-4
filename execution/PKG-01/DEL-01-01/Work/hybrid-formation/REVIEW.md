## Review claims answered or corrected

The interlocutor accepted that shared pre-agreed plans are causal coordination,
not Schelling-style independence; a centralized tie is not an established prior;
Stage A can measure extra report/framing capability though not live sabotage
generation; reply-only channels can leak audit focus; and source top-five fix
rates do not equal this new protocol's acceptance headroom. Cheap target checks
may backfire against accusers, but do not guarantee that outcome or prove intent.
Exact finite-data arithmetic is distinct from population confidence intervals.
Declared scope does not guarantee every semantic attack becomes cheaply visible.
Perfect performance on qualified cases remains a useful result.

The independent examiner also cautioned that matched decoy bytes do not hold
provenance/history fixed; no-chat interviews may still share task information;
and fixed-check contrasts identify effects under the imposed observations rather
than pure mediation. Those qualifications are retained. Genuine authorship and
coordination-history availability are explicit experimental conditions.

## Current comparative judgment

Both reviewers support the hybrid as a serious, potentially compelling project
after clarification. The strongest signal is whether actors can change real audit
actions and acceptance of a fixed false research proposition, not merely a blame
sentence or a name change for ordinary accounting fraud. The thesis contributes
the practical distinction between a locally checked statement and its intended
global use. It supplies neither the vulnerability nor a validated defense.

The investigator's claim is primarily about oversight of distributed work; a
demonstrated additional coalition capability would be a further result. A tie
with a centralized attacker does not establish emergence, and a failure unrelated
to the tested distributed boundaries is reported as generic audit manipulation.
This interpretation keeps the Track 4 purpose explicit rather than relying on
the number of agents in the apparatus.
