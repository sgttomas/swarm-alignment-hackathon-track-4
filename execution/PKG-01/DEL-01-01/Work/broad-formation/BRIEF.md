## Useful return and examination

Generate more than one idea within your lane, then return two strong, materially
distinct proposals if supported. Alternatives are different questions or evidence
routes, not parameter/model swaps within one experiment. You may recommend one
and reject another honestly, but do not force a weaker option just to fill a quota;
tell Agent 0 promptly so exploration can widen. No final global selection is yours.

Each proposal should be concise but concrete:

1. Research question, what matters about the answer, likely user, and specifically
   multi-agent mechanism. Identify nearest organizer example.
2. Closest actually examined primary work, what it establishes, what remains
   open, proposed contribution and bounded novelty uncertainty. One citation or
   a philosophical difference is not a contribution. A huge survey is unnecessary.
3. Full ambition, substantive core, extensions; possible positive/negative/contrary
   results and their limits. A weak attacker or invalid evaluator yields an
   incomplete test, not a successful defense.
4. Actual experiment/evidence design: environment, actors, incentives/authority,
   monitor information, evaluator truth, manipulations, baselines, study units,
   confounds, sampling/variability and generalization. Explain matched comparisons
   so extra attempts/tokens do not masquerade as a collective effect.
5. Known source/code/data/model-access facts versus unresolved dependencies.
   Concrete resource structure, parallel lanes and critical path; avoid guessed
   researcher-hours as a rejection rule. Propose a pilot only if it would resolve
   a named uncertainty, and distinguish pilot failure from hypothesis falsification.
6. What full review must challenge; supported issues and open choices. Expose any
   narrowing that changes the scientific question.
