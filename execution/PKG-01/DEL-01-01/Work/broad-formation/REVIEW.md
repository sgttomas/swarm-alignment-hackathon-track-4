## Reviewer claims corrected rather than adopted

The interlocutor withdrew the claim that a finite equal-budget single model must
match a coalition unless oversight is indexed by agent. Decomposition, specialist
contexts and search organization can differ; identity-indexed oversight is one
testable mechanism rather than a necessity theorem. Same-decomposition baselines
were nevertheless useful additions.

The interlocutor also withdrew the forecast that option 1 would likely be
incomplete from rerun cost, the assertion that shared plans force a null in hidden
reviewer assignment, and the assertion that an honest team member makes capability
recovery trivial. Competent attacks, a transferable signal and reachable skill
remain empirical conditions. Unknown cost does not remove an ambitious option.

The interlocutor further accepted that consistent fake capability profiles do
not identify coordination: independent workers can follow the same predetermined
failure policy. That baseline is now explicit. Mathew's learned task-choice
carriers also corrected the interlocutor's initial claim that such carriers were
new; the proposal's contribution remains their workflow-level control evaluation.

Missing paraphrase literature does not imply every contribution in option 8 needs
GPU access: the structured semantic arm can be API-based. Canonicalization itself
is not new. Two distinct model families already permit a heterogeneous assignment
contrast; three is not a logical prerequisite. More families improve scope.

The interlocutor's proposed ambiguity measures (report-free diagnosis dispersion
and number of effective repairs) are possible diagnostic covariates, not truth
labels. The revised option 9 fixes strata independently of attack outcomes and
does not confuse nonunique responsibility with missing records. Evaluator-framing
is optional; abstention was already present.
