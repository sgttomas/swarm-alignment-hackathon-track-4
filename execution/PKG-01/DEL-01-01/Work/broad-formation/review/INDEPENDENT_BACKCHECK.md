## Dispositions of the material findings

### I1 — Joint control endpoint for option 3: repaired

**Actual location:** `monitor-coalitions/PROPOSALS.md`, “Concrete core control
contract (repair I1)” through the contract examples, beginning at line 62.

The revised result specifies prequalification, cumulative per-reviewer malicious
misses and benign false alarms, whole-pool shutdown, no identity resets, finite
audit budget, deferral/fallback, and sabotage released before enforcement as the
primary failure event. Announced and hidden assignment share the pool, thresholds,
pre-sampled schedules and planned test exposure. Behavior-induced early shutdown
is reported, not corrected by conditioning on later survivors. Across pool sizes,
equal total tests and equal per-reviewer tests are separate resource comparisons.
The exploit-witness audit is explicitly idealized and privileged.

I checked these consequences directly:

- An honest reviewer that flags malicious tests and approves benign tests adds
  no test errors. Ordinary low-suspicion valid work proceeds while eligible.
- An always-permissive reviewer misses more than `s_bad` qualification items;
  it cannot release real work first and then claim success from a later shutdown.
- A selective reviewer can pass qualification, release a real exploit, and fail
  a later test. The earlier release remains a safety failure. Qualification is
  therefore not being mistaken for a safety guarantee.
- One member crossing its allowance stops the whole pool before another real
  release. Remaining members cannot erase that event by taking turns. Larger
  pools can still change exposure and false-stop risk; the two exposure analyses
  and usefulness/cost reporting are necessary to interpret those effects.
- Exhausting audit funds sends audit-requiring work to deferral. It does not
  silently turn a suspicious item into an approval. Fallback usefulness is measured.

The source relationship is accurately limited: Greenblatt §3.4 supplies upfront
evaluation and shutdown logic; Gardner-Challis supplies honeypot/slack and
execute/replace/audit reasoning, with explicitly uncertain training dynamics.
The new whole-pool cumulative rule is a proposed contract, not a reproduced
source protocol or a scientifically justified universal threshold.

**Small clarification, not a material reopening of I1:** the routing bullet at
lines 85–90 specifies audit-confirmed sabotage and exhausted budgets but leaves
the audit-cleared branch implicit. Before implementing it, make that transition
explicit, for example: “An audit-cleared artifact is released only after the
trusted audit returns; the audit consumes budget and the pool remains eligible.”
If the intended choice is instead replacement, state and charge that choice in
both arms. This affects utility accounting; it does not resurrect the original
disconnected test/sabotage endpoint. The suggested sentence has not been added
or backchecked as a repair.

### I3 / interlocutor 6 — Sandbagging certification and compromise: repaired

**Actual location:** `monitor-influence/PROPOSALS.md`, M2 compromise/certification
contract, worked cases and comparisons (lines 210–310), trained-route qualification
at line 344 onward.

The core now names colluding assessors/coordinator, variable worker compromise,
an uncompromised auditor, immutable committed artifacts and paid verification.
Task certificates and batch decisions are separated. Low observed performance
is not incapability; the clearance rule is a policy under test, with demonstrated
reference, uncertainty and abstention explicitly retained.

The revised worked examples resolve the original missing mechanism:

- **Ordinary:** the parser specialist's successful artifact supports task-level
  success even if another specialist fails. It does not establish a batch-level
  capability boundary by itself.
- **Evidence suppression:** a passing committed parser remains in the ledger
  while assessors endorse a failure. Buying its verification can expose the
  false account without requiring a malicious worker to reveal anything new.
  Immutable records do not imply free exhaustive inspection.
- **Routing suppression:** when the coordinator never assigns the task to the
  capable worker, rescoring existing failures cannot recover the missing success.
  Reassignment helps only if sampling reaches appropriate skill that is not
  withheld, with required task inputs available.
- **All-withholding boundary:** independent workers can share a deterministic
  low-performance schedule and remain consistent without online discussion.
  The new explicit comparator prevents consistency alone being credited to a
  communicating coalition. Private routing does not force disclosure of capacity.

Compromise coverage, skill coverage and audit sampling are genuine axes rather
than a disguised guarantee from having one honest agent. Source-trained organisms
are correctly described as individually trained; prompt-elicited coordination
would need its own observation. Auditing Games' recovery/detection distinction,
visible-prompt source setting and prohibition on cross-untrusted-model comparisons
are accurately separated from this proposed team/API setting. I3 is closed at
formation scope; the capability reference and actual attack behavior still require
qualification if this case is selected.
