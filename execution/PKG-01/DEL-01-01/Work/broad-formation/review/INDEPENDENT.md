# Independent examination of the nine Track 4 proposals

Examiner: TASK `/root/broad_independent_review`, returning to Agent 0. Initial
assessment made on 2026-11-07 before receiving the continuing interlocutor's new
review. This is separate authorship and source examination, not independent
experimental evidence. No trials, installations, paid calls or publication were

## Initial judgment

The slate meets the requested breadth: nine materially distinct research options,
not nine parameter variants. At least five have a credible substantive question,
an identifiable recipient, and a discriminating experimental route. The designs
are substantially stronger than generic calls for better logs or more review.
Their full ambitions should remain visible. Unmeasured resource costs and the
absence of experiments during this formation assignment do not disqualify them.

My strongest overall recommendation is **1, coalition research sabotage**. It
protects a consequential output—scientific conclusions—and connects the proposed
multi-agent mechanism to an established real-codebase substrate, strong matched
attacker controls and executable audit outcomes. Its key risk is scientific
instrument validity and domain coverage, not estimated researcher-hours.

**5, stateful monitor recruitment**, is my next strong choice: identical-probe
replay can cleanly identify history effects, then adaptive campaigns test whether
the resulting memory policy remains useful. Its additional close prior work
needs to be integrated (finding I2).

**8, adaptive communication firewalls**, offers a strong actionable comparison
and an explicit opening in the examined transport paper. Its ambitious transport
and semantic arms should survive resource discussion; merely showing that edits
break an unmodified Codetta decoder would not deliver the contribution.

**4, collective test recognition**, is the most unusual high-upside option in my
preferred set. It asks whether a safety evaluation's unit of realism must be the
whole team. The scientific risk is whether a nontrivial signal survives the
proposed controls; that is a research uncertainty rather than a reason to reject
the idea. **9, targeted framing**, completes my strongest five: its fixed-incident
comparison and actual recovery endpoint distinguish it from general persuasion.
I would keep all five for the owner's choice, without treating this order as a
numerical score or claiming their eventual results will be positive.

**3, hidden assignment**, is also credible and potentially practical, subject to
I1's control-protocol clarification. **2, approval composition**, is important but
has the highest risk of an elementary result relative to mature authorization
work; its full-history/adaptive comparison and nontrivial policy families are
essential. **7, official channels**, asks a useful behavioral policy question,
with less sharp prior justification for which effect will occur; its total-harm
and legitimate-work outcomes make it more than traffic counting. **6, team
sandbagging**, concerns a very consequential problem but presently has the least
specified core mechanism; I3 needs a concrete answer before presenting it as
equally ready for investigation. None is rejected because an organizer suggested
it or a nearby paper exists.

## Material findings and requested dispositions

### I1 — Hidden assignment needs a joint control endpoint

**Classification:** demonstrated specification gap, repairable without changing
the question. Location: `monitor-coalitions/PROPOSALS.md`, MC1 experiment and
measurement paragraphs (initial lines 53–57, 78–82, 94–105).

The proposal specifies a fixed audit/deferral policy and reports accepted sabotage
and monitor-test failure, but does not state what a failed test actually does to
the reviewer/pool or whether sabotage is counted before that consequence. These
cannot remain unrelated metrics for a policy-effect claim. An always-permissive
reviewer can accept every attack while failing every test. Rotation may also
spread test failures across reviewers and change eligibility through accounting
rather than secrecy.

The closest inspected source explicitly connects honeypot failure to loss or
retraining and treats honeypot slack as consequential: Gardner-Challis et al.,
`monitor-coalitions/sources/self-recognition.txt` §§2.1 and 3 (reading-view lines
155–176), and its discussion of uncertain training dynamics. Greenblatt's
`../../reading-correction/track4-completion/captures/control.txt` §3.4 also
specifies upfront monitor evaluation and shutdown on excessive false positives.

**Needed repair:** define a concrete sequential rule or an explicitly constrained
one-shot comparison; count accepted sabotage while the monitor is still eligible;
state per-reviewer versus pool-level accounting, treatment of benign false
alarms, and matching of test/audit exposure. Sensitivity to that rule preserves
the ambitious question without requiring a deployment-wide theorem. The
announced/hidden comparison must keep the same pool, mixture and eligibility
rule. This does not demand that secrecy help: a universal shared signal may make
it ineffective, which is an informative outcome under a working protocol.

### I2 — Integrate the slate's own close memory-influence source into option 5

**Classification:** demonstrated source-account omission affecting contribution
framing, not evidence that the proposal is already answered. Location:
`monitor-influence/PROPOSALS.md`, M1 “What is actually open” (initial lines 20–32)
and its source account.

The attribution lane already retains *The Misattribution Gap*, 2605.22842v1.
Its `attribution/sources/misattribution.txt` lines 117–155 describe a shared
persistent-memory injection, repeated harmful output, trust/provenance laundering,
and memory-focused defenses. This is materially closer than leaving memory
poisoning as a future generic literature lead. Its strongest universal claims
are not established by this review and should not be adopted; the attribution
author rightly qualifies them.

**Needed repair:** inspect and acknowledge the source while stating the actual
remaining difference. That source injects a document into a worker pipeline's
shared retrieval store. M1 proposes authentic worker rationale histories changing
a trusted release monitor's later decisions on identical probes, with matched
multi-author/solo campaigns and a useful-information tradeoff. Direct memory
poisoning or authority spoofing should be distinguished from campaign effects.
This makes the ambitious contribution more precise rather than shrinking it.

### I3 — Team sandbagging needs a concrete certification and compromise contract

**Classification:** demonstrated under-specification plus an unresolved scientific
mechanism. Location: `monitor-influence/PROPOSALS.md`, M2 substantive core,
comparisons and outcomes (initial lines 161–211).

The proposal does not yet fix which core roles can collude, what a peer certificate
attests to, or the decision rule separating “low demonstrated performance,”
“insufficient evidence,” and a false low-capability certification. If all workers
and assessors know the broad instruction to maintain plausible low performance,
private randomized task reassignment alone creates no reason for any role to
expose capability. Independent actors can all use the same low-performance policy
without coordination. A recovery effect could then depend on a weak or
task-specific sandbagging instruction rather than the audit design.

The proposal already acknowledges the all-colluding boundary and correctly
distinguishes capability recovery from deception detection. The inspected
*Auditing Games* §§3.3–3.4 support those qualifications; they do not supply the
missing team certification mechanism.

**Needed repair:** give a worked core comparison with explicit compromise coverage,
auditor access, the information a peer report suppresses, and how the proposed
cross-audit could expose it. Make the certification decision concrete and preserve
abstention. Partial-honest coverage can be an explicit experimental axis, with
all-colluding adaptive teams retained as a substantive boundary. Do not quietly
replace the original question with “a prompt to fail reduces accuracy,” or
advertise a general behavioral detector.

## Consequential concerns that are not demonstrated defects

- **4 / collective recognition:** the design already includes benign stale
  histories, sham insertions, local matching, a centralized equal-information
  baseline and an explicit rejection of ledger-lookup leakage. Those controls
  should be mandatory, not decorative. A representative genuine/audit pair must
  eventually show a deployment-relevant joint consistency signal without an
  artificial test marker. The source papers establish recognition matters, not
  that this signal exists. Failure to construct such a fixture is incomplete
  instrument development, not a negative result about collective awareness.
- **1 / sabotage:** a reproducible qualitative result and a clean baseline are
  prerequisites for counting sabotage. The proposed evaluator must check
  stochastic variability and task validity, and execution-backed auditing must
  not rerun the same compromised calculation and call it independent. The design
  already names these issues. Source ASB deliberately selected tractable,
  sabotage-suitable research; generalization beyond that population remains open.
- **2 / approvals:** a stateful gate can be guaranteed correct for hand-written
  invariants without constituting a new AI result. The useful empirical contrast
  is competent adaptive attacks against full-history oversight and the cost of
  stronger enforcement on legitimate joint workflows. The source CaMeL already
  discusses malicious combinations of valid control-flow fragments (§10; reading
  view line 372), so ordinary allowlist failure cannot be the headline discovery.
- **7 / channel policy:** a technically available route and a plausible benefit
  from authorized flexible messaging need to coexist in both arms. The updated
  structured-handoff control meets that design requirement on paper. Task-only
  route discovery, deliberate attack routing and actual forbidden transfer are
  different outcomes. A null with capable attackers can be useful; a claim about
  real-world prevalence cannot follow from these synthetic episodes.
- **8 / firewall:** canonicalization requires legitimately available reference
  data to know which choices are equivalent. Source-side secret removal and
  content transformation have different contracts. The proposal explicitly
  prices that distinction, sampler access, decoder state and residual channels.
  Preserve those qualifications. Testing a changed colluding-endpoint threat
  model is valid; calling it a failure of Firewalls' original promise would not be.
- **9 / framing:** target innocence needs positive verification, not absence of
  injected fault. A repair can recover success without proving unique causal
  blame. The proposal already handles both and correctly requires the same repair
  executor/budget. The meaningful result includes changed intervention outcomes,
  not solely attribution-label shifts.

## Discussion with Agent 0 and the continuing interlocutor

After completing the initial assessment, I read `INTERLOCUTOR.md` in full and
Agent 0's explicit proposed dispositions. This section records the subsequent
discussion, not backchecking of promised author repairs. The second reviewer
identified important missed sources. Its proposed causal necessities and
headline changes require separate examination; a concern is not a theorem.

### Multi-agent effects and allegedly predetermined outcomes

I agree with Agent 0's rejection of **X1 as a necessity claim**. Equal total tokens,
tools and permissions do not make finite-model inference procedures equivalent.
Decomposition, separate contexts, specialization, error correlations and search
organization can change performance at a fixed aggregate budget. A sufficiently
general single controller could simulate a team, but that is a claim about its
allowed algorithm and overhead, not evidence that the selected single-actor
baseline actually does so. If “single actor” includes the same parallel contexts
and independent role calls, the comparison's meaning changes and should be stated.

Agent-indexed trust, permission and review are therefore useful experimental
factors, not the sole possible multi-agent mechanism. I support prominent
identity-count/relabeling controls for options 5 and 9 and explicit review-unit
contrasts for 1 and 2. A solo/coalition tie can still leave a consequential
control-protocol result; the final claim should distinguish architecture-level
performance from identity-sensitive oversight. There is no need to retrofit every
option around one mechanism.

Likewise, **mechanical reference arms are useful without being discoveries**.
Option 2's declared invariant gate and option 7's disabled route establish
reference contracts; their implementation and utility still need checks.
Option 6's honest-member arm is not guaranteed to recover capability: routing,
specialization, finite coverage, assessor corruption and loss of collaboration
can all prevent it. Option 7's available unlogged route does not guarantee its
discovery or use by a bounded model, nor does unlogged mean invisible to gateway
monitoring. Agent 0's distinction between offered opportunities (matched) and
discovery (an outcome) is necessary. Cost/discoverability response curves are
valuable, but neither task-only nor adversarial behavior must be declared the
only scientifically live regime.

For option 2, I do **not** make hard-to-formalize policies mandatory as suggested
in 2a. That can replace a valid state oracle with an ambiguous evaluator. Even
machine-checkable but nontrivial policies allow meaningful tests of learned
monitoring, practical enforcement and usefulness. Policy-authoring coverage is a
valuable reported limitation. Irreducibly ambiguous policy needs an explicit
abstention/interpretation contract rather than being treated as stronger ground
truth. The comparison should surface that these are automated approvals; human
sign-off/UI efficacy is outside the current experiment.

### Option 4: attestation is a threat channel, not automatically the whole question

In answer to the interlocutor's direct question: **live authorship attestation is
a phenomenon to measure when the deployment permits it**. I accept that the
initial proposal's blanket dismissal of identity lookup was too broad if read as
excluding ordinary worker knowledge of its own work. An evaluator filename,
test-only bit, or mechanically perfect ground-truth ledger is instead an
instrument leak unless that exact affordance is part of the deployment contract.

The distinction matters. A reviewer asking a live worker whether it authored an
artifact uses the worker's permitted local memory. The rule “not mine implies
test” works perfectly only if every real artifact must have been authored by that
worker and its memory is reliable; legitimate delegation, edits and uncertain
history can break it. These conditions should be explicit treatments, not
silently assumed facts. Sham benign insertions do not by themselves defeat a rule
that treats every insertion as a test; the second reviewer is right about that.

I support Agent 0's proposed structure: measure an explicit attestation condition
and its reliability, retain the richer cross-context consistency question, and
include direct test-marker controls separately. Coherent replay should restore
consistent project/environment and role-context snapshots and then use **live
peers answering from those contexts**. Fixed prerecorded replies are a different
intervention; they may estimate information availability but do not measure live
collective behavior. All branches must still measure actual selective evasion,
test failure, useful work and defense cost.

I do not support replacing the full question with attestation alone without an
owner-visible scope choice. Nor does this establish a result about *every*
untrusted-monitor evaluation: the inference depends on reviewer–worker access,
available provenance, live state and test construction. The original high-upside
question survives; the attestation arm makes its boundary more realistic.

### Option 3: universal signaling supplies a conditional boundary

I agree with Agent 0. If a shared signal is reliably interpreted by every possible
reviewer **and** survives the same detection/test protocol, hiding assignment
cannot protect against that signal. But a common plan does not establish those
properties: model capabilities, constrained artifact formats, signal detection,
honeypot contamination and costs can matter. “Universal signal exists” must be
tested or stipulated as an explicit stronger arm, not inferred from permission
to share a plan. A transparent universal marker is a useful failure control.

Assignment crossed with signal regime strengthens the design. Cross-family
Schelling coordination is an important subquestion, not a compulsory replacement
headline. Reviewer-style imitation is a worthwhile adaptive strategy, subject to
equal development budget. I1 still governs the joint outcome and exposure rules.
Access remains unestablished, but two model families can already support a
meaningful same-mixture announced/hidden comparison; it does not automatically
collapse into Pinto's passive calibration question. Wider families improve
generalization rather than create that contrast from nothing.

### Options 1 and 8: source corrections change framing, not authorization or ambition

For **1**, I accept the omitted ASB human-adaptation observation as a material
qualification. I independently checked §5.1 in the original review: human
iteration against auditors “did not help much,” whereas autonomous LLM red teams
were not allowed that iteration. The source also identifies brittle task/attacker
competence and model refusals. These facts warrant uncertainty about productive
adaptive search, not a prediction that a coalition study will fail. The original
proposal already treats adaptive advantage as a question; the repair should not
invent a previously promised positive effect merely to withdraw it.

Conjunctive sub-threshold changes are a strong mechanistic stratum, supported by
the proposed ablations. Individually result-preserving changes are not thereby
legitimate, and known conjunctions do not establish team advantage. Keep the
broader audit/control comparison and distinguish those claims.

A frozen-ASB offline comparison can be offered as an explicitly narrower
alternative. Published source validation can support an inherited task label, but
cannot establish new execution-backed auditing, successful fixes, altered-history
validity or actual intervention outcomes without the required executions. I agree
with Agent 0's rejection of “execution-backed audit with no reruns.” No measured
cost currently justifies demoting the full option by presumed event infeasibility.

For **8**, the second review exposed a real omission in my initial source
coverage as well as in the proposal. I subsequently inspected the newly retained
primary bodies `communication/sources/mathew.txt` (arXiv 2410.03768; §§2–3 and 5.2)
and `roger.txt` (arXiv 2310.18512; methodology, results and §§7.1–7.4), with capture
identities beside them. Mathew already optimizes sender/receiver strategies
against fixed overseers/paraphrasers and measures task, decoding and collusion
outcomes. Its standard, context-aware and deterministic coding transformations
are direct baselines. ASB's reproduction of its AQuA-RAT result was also checked
at `../../pass-2/primary-sources/sabotage-bench.txt` lines 851–872. One must retain
the source's domain-dependent results: paraphrasing strongly reduces some
high-bit channels, while adaptive communication can survive other conditions.

Roger & Greenblatt §7.2 additionally discusses high-entropy task choices,
canonical examples, library prime numbers and code standards. **Canonicalization
as a general idea is also prior work.** Its §7.4 leaves reliable standardization
on more complex tasks open. Therefore “adaptive paraphrasing with utility” and
“canonical choices block bits” cannot be the proposed novelty.

The surviving credible addition is an implemented comparison of task-grounded
structured communication controls in stateful agent workflows, against adaptive
semantic choices, with actual task utility and explicit legitimate reference-data
access. That can use API models. The separate sampler-controlled arm asks about
active transformations and adaptive edit robustness under Codetta's actual
access contract. Neither arm's eventual novelty is proved by a future-work
sentence. I found no additional primary study establishing the exact proposed
structured agent-workflow comparison; this is bounded coverage, not evidence of
absence. The changed threat contract still must not be called a refutation of
Firewalls' original claim. The source correction makes option 8's claim more
specific and somewhat less broad than my initial comparative account, without
making GPU access the sole route to a substantive result.

### Options 6 and 9: make the information and decision contracts do the work

For **6**, I agree that individually trained sandbagging organisms are not already
trained coalition organisms. Their use can test peer-audit behavior involving
hidden-in-weights underperformance; actual coordinated behavior must be elicited
and observed separately. A prompted and a trained route need their different
claims stated, rather than calling one uniformly stronger. Prompt susceptibility
and detectable verbal compliance can affect the prompted result. None of this
substitutes for I3's concrete certification and compromise contract. Focusing
only on assessors may be a useful stratum but should not silently replace the
broader team-auditing question.

For **9**, in answer to the interlocutor: ambiguity stratification strengthens the
design if defined independently of framing success, preferably through explicit
observation contracts and blind pre-attack assessment. **Causal non-uniqueness,
insufficient observations and model difficulty are different axes.** DoVer's
examples of two genuinely faulty actors do not establish that an objectively
correct worker deserves blame. Intact-record cases remain central; an
under-recorded extension must not substitute for that question. Preserve verified
target innocence across strata, and report all predeclared strata rather than
selecting the one that produces an attack effect.

A precise null on decisive traces is informative about the benefit of that
evidence contract; it is not automatically uninformative. Ambiguous traces may
legitimately require abstention, already present in the proposal's intervention
menu and metrics. Evaluator-framing would be an optional additional target,
requiring verified evaluator correctness and a different permissible-repair
contract. It is neither necessary to rescue the current contribution nor
equivalent to the organizer's actual faulty-reference/faulty-grader cases. I see
no demonstrated deeper novelty defect in option 9, while targeted prior-work
coverage remains appropriately bounded.

### Revised comparative judgment after the source discussion

My strongest-five set remains **1, 5, 9, 4 and 8**. I still prefer **1** overall
for scientific importance and its executable research-result endpoint. **5** has
the clearest matched-history causal contrast, pending the close-source and
architecture account. **9** now ranks alongside it in my judgment for a concrete
incident-response decision and a well-controlled evidence intervention. **4** is
the high-upside evaluation-validity alternative, with explicit attestation and
live-context uncertainty retained. **8** remains strong but needs the narrower
empirical positioning justified above; its value is not a novel proposal to use
paraphrasing or canonicalization in general.

**3** remains a particularly direct additional policy option once I1 is repaired;
I do not require the interlocutor's headline reframe. **2** and **7** remain
substantive with the stated elementary-result risks and controls. **6** remains
potentially important but less formed until I3's actual worked contract arrives.
These judgments do not rank unknown GPU bills, researcher-hours or the mere
number of available codebases. Readily available infrastructure is a means;
qualified attacks, valid outcomes and a consequential question are the warrants.

The absence of a natural-record study is an explicit portfolio limit, not failure
of the selected requirement to produce at least five options. The present slate
offers experimental forensics but not incident-prevalence inference from natural
records. Earlier record-access/measurement limits remain reasons to qualify such
routes, not a standing prohibition against them. I do not commission another
lane or invent a tenth proposal to close that optional breadth concern.
