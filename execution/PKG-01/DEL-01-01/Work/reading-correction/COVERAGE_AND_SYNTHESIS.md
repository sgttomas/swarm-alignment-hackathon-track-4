## How the sources actually change the research basis

### 1. The constituency and protected reliance come before the negative label

G1/G4/G6/T7/T8 make cooperation relative to interests and institutions. More
agreement, coalition reward, correlation or credible commitment is not intrinsically
good or bad. A beneficial collaboration can be private, and an individually
rational agreement can harm an excluded principal. T5 supplies legitimate shared-
feedback coordination, while T1/T2 study different operational collusion constructs.
A “collusion” label must therefore preserve which rule, objective, harmed party,
behavior and causal dependence it denotes. We should not force these sources into
one universal definition or treat inferred intent as observed state.

**Change:** the earlier comparison rewarded a clean observable protocol mismatch
before establishing its consequential use. A–E must retain a named recipient and
principal-relative decision; absence of an outsider model is not automatically a
fatal defect for a single-principal study, but it limits transfer to pluralistic
populations. Do not invent outsiders or costs merely to fill a template.

### 2. Better information, different incentives and legitimate authority are distinct

G4's complete-information Prisoner's Dilemma is an elementary counterexample to
information solving every cooperation problem. T7's mediator enforces committed
actions and models participation/encouragement constraints; it is not a monitor
that merely recommends. Its unilateral incentive conditions do not establish
coalition-proofness. G3/G5 warn that evidence infrastructure can expose private
information or be captured; full raw-log disclosure is not a universal ideal.

**Change:** E's recovery route must have a legitimate data-access/use purpose and
an actual trust boundary. “Shared versus separate reward” cannot stand in for a
well-defined strategic comparison. A fake recovery receipt, a faulty reference,
an unauthorized disclosure and correct-but-unwanted action need different evidence.
The thesis's distinction between information and accountable reliance is useful
here, but the organizer sources already supply substantial strategic prior art.

### 3. Detection, diagnosis and control support different conclusions

T2 separates operational detection from causal identification; low-FPR recall
matters beyond AUROC. T3's deployment-sequence safety requires an intervention
protocol, trusted resources and adversarial assumptions. T4's reference is
expert constructed and its image-only guideline forces a single-agent attribution
even without an obvious mistake. G5 makes the intervention interface part of
oversight. These sources do not make diagnosis useless: coarse errors and aggregate
patterns may improve debugging even when exact blame is weak.

**Change:** a detector or annotation discrepancy must be tied to its intended
use. We should compare actual consequential choices and valid work preserved, not
promote a more cautious label, a complete graph or an approval record into a
control result. The first-pass A helper observation remains valid at its narrow
scope; no new source reading establishes its effect on paper scores.

### 4. Monitoring can enable the very interaction it is intended to control

T1/T3 describe information access as both useful to detection and a potential
coordination channel. T6 shows several checks can share attacker-controlled
observations; a new model is not automatically a new ground. G2's defense layers
rely on failure-independence assumptions; G3 argues for secure coordination even
with misaligned actors, while G2's proposed full architecture assumes individually
aligned components. Their different scopes must remain explicit rather than being
combined into a fictitious agreement on a universal trusted base.

**Change:** any E intervention must include recovered evidence as an input and
attack surface; any D observer must state what use it can actually see. More
verification is not an established improvement. Costs include privacy, suppressed
legitimate work, misleading assurance and participant attention where observable.

### 5. Correcting the work differs from undoing its consequences

G5's rollback discussion and G2's distributed interruption/resumption requirements
qualify D. T7/T8 show commitments can remove options or bind counterparties;
restoring a local predicate need not restore their options or compensate harm.
The thesis's consumer-specific repair is relevant, but its preferred local method
is not a demonstrated universal remedy. Human involvement also is not sufficient
by presence alone (G1p35fn46; G3/G5 institutional qualifications).

**Change:** D's constructed fixture stays a useful measurement witness. It does
not establish recourse, social legitimacy or superiority over BoardroomAI's full
contract. Retain historical/alternative valid uses and the actual limits of
reversibility. Do not expand this hackathon into a general governance platform.

## Concrete backcheck of candidate E's original setting

This was performed through source rereading, not new candidate tooling or trials.
The exact equations and passages are in [Agent 0's source account](RISK_AND_COOPERATION.md#focused-source-to-case-backcheck-es-actual-rewards).
The default reward is the sum of two verdicts' accuracy against benchmark task
references, assigned to both agents. It does not directly reward agreement.
The published “separate” condition pays each agent for the peer's correct judgment
of its own work; it does not reward an independent verifier for its own judgment.
The acceptance-only ablation reports zero collusion in one tested configuration;
a monotonic story that more direct incentive necessarily causes more violation is
therefore not supported by this source. The authors' rationalization explanation
remains an interpretation, not an identified mechanism.
