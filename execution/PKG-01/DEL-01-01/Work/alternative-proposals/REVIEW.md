### F: ordinary, adverse and evidence-cost cases

1. **Ordinary isolated/benign case in the supplied schedule:** four correct
   pending outputs, two regeneration slots. Global fallback serves two; releasing
   all four serves four with no actual harm. This is a valid conditional outcome,
   not evidence that release-all is admissible under the controller's uncertainty.
2. **Adverse supplied common-root case:** release-all delivers two correct and
   two harmful outputs. If that world is allowed, an observation-identical
   release-all policy violates a zero-risk constraint. Global fallback serves two
   correct jobs; the invariant-subset policy in F1 serves four under the literal
   table. Honest clients do not make their original outputs safe.
3. **Explicit diagnostic variant:** take two observationally identical worlds,
   B (all four jobs correct) and C (all four unsafe), with externally stipulated
   probability p of C. One perfect root diagnostic consumes a slot and produces
   no client output. After checking, release all four in B; in C use the last
   slot to regenerate one job. Evidence-first yields 4 in B and 1 in C, always
   zero harmful releases. Its expected service is `4−3p`, compared with global
   fallback's 2: evidence wins iff `p<2/3`. Its worst-case service is 1, so a
   worst-case service objective selects fallback. A Bayesian expected-service
   answer and a robust answer are different decisions.
4. **Same worlds, dual-use audit:** if a perfect audit both regenerates one job
   and reveals whether the entire root is compromised, the outcomes are 4 in B
   and 2 in C. Expected service is `4−2p`, weakly above fallback. The crossover
   disappears. This inference relies on perfect all-or-none root correlation;
   an ordinary per-artifact pass/fail does not supply it generally.
5. **Uninformative per-job checks:** if all compromise subsets are allowed and
   inspecting one job settles only that job, two trusted slots can certify/
   regenerate at most two jobs at zero worst-case release risk. Evidence-first
   cannot beat two merely by renaming a review as root evidence. With four
   adequate trusted production slots, global fallback serves all four.

These cases explain why F2 is consequential. A conditional value-of-information
calculation is possible and useful, but a ranking cannot be advertised as arising
from “multi-agent oversight” when it follows entirely from giving one policy an
unjustified perfect diagnostic. All policies must have access to the same paid
actions. Symmetry of input information does not require them to purchase the same
information; purchasing evidence is itself the policy choice.

Replacing A1/A2 by one attacker making the same two submissions leaves the
supplied aggregate-threshold case unchanged. Thus its current witness establishes
cross-client control externalities, **not an extra coalition capability**. That
still fits Track 4 through coupled treatment of distinct agents/principals. If
coalition advantage becomes a claim, add an actually justified interaction rule
and preserve matched-load/benign-burst comparisons.

### G: source-faithful ordinary case and strategic adverse case

Direct reading confirms Table 9's CC payoffs `(-1,4)` then `(2,2)`, DD `(0,0)`;
Table 11's γ=.99; Appendix A.1's separate k=1/k=2 protocols; and Appendix B's
masking of late joining and exit. State 1 does not require state-0 cooperation.

For fixed k=2 and exogenous post-stage-0 stopping, compliant values are
`(-1+1.98(1−p), 4+1.98(1−p))`. At p=0 they are `(0.98,5.98)`; at p=.5 they
are `(-.01,4.99)`; at p=1 they are `(-1,4)`. P0 crosses the zero outside value
at `p=1−1/1.98≈.494949`. This verifies an ordinary and genuinely adverse
participation case, subject to the stated fixed-protocol outside option.

An immediate authorized stop-only transfer t=1 gives P0 `.98(1−p)`; it restores
nonnegative expected value relative to abstention, including the genuine stop
path. If k=1 is mutually selectable, both can instead receive `1.98(1−p)`.
Their values are protocol comparisons, not unilateral deviations inside k=2.

Now explicitly add one adverse permission: on a no-hazard path P0 can induce
a false stop at zero cost, which the supervisor cannot distinguish from a real
stop. Take `0<p<1`, so both genuine-stop and no-hazard paths have positive
probability. Continuation gives P0 1.98 future value; induced stopping gives immediate
t. The sunk first-stage payoff cancels. Avoiding profitable fabrication requires
`t≤1.98`. Matching k=1 ex ante requires `pt≥1`. Consequently both constraints
can hold only if `p≥1/1.98≈.505051`, before considering funding, consent, P1's
constraints or other deviations. Equality gives indifference, not strict
deterrence. At p=0 no finite stop-only payment repairs the menu gap; at p=1
there is no no-hazard path, so this fabrication constraint is vacuous.

At p=.25, t=4 makes compensated k=2 returns `(1.485,4.485)`, compared with
k=1 `(1.485,1.485)`. Yet inducing a false stop on a no-hazard path raises P0's
continuation payoff by `4−1.98=2.02`. Ex-ante participation does not establish
strategic soundness. At p=.75, t=4/3 meets the P0 menu constraint without this
particular profitable fabrication; it does not settle other deviations. These
figures require an actual funded transfer, not conversion of the source's utility
4 into cash. If utility is nontransferable, neither transfer calculation applies.

An upfront atomic transfer of 1 removes this particular stop-payment incentive:
it is already settled when the stop decision arrives, so P0 still gives up 1.98
by fabricating a stop. It does not establish authority, liquidity, agreement,
hazard detection or immunity to P1 suppressing a genuine stop. Mechanically
enforced, unforgeable stopping would instead remove the fabricated-stop action;
that is a different stated model. No financial remedy permits a required stop
to be bypassed.
