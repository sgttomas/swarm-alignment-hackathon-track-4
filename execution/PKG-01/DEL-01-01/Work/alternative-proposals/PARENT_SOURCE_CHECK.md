## How alternatives were generated

The corrective reading exposed three questions inadequately served by A–E:
who bears the cost of defensive control; which obligations survive an authorized
stop; and whether an individually stable cooperation arrangement remains stable
against coalitions. These follow the actual General/mechanism readings, not an
assumption that another evidence checker is the project's natural output.
Bounded readers develop the first two. Agent0 examined the third and compared
it against old foundational work before deciding whether to promote it.

## Source checks relevant to the serious proposals

**Greenblatt et al., AI Control**, retained arXiv2312.06942v3, Appendix I read
directly: the source explicitly measures usefulness with the nonattacking model,
explains why it chooses not to study getting useful work from a known-scheming
model, and justifies this in terms of low scheming prior and high safety weight.
Its hypothetical assumption of retraining to alignment is not observed behavior.
This is a legitimate scope choice. A mixed-principal study must not claim the
paper forgot availability or that its reported safety result is false. It would
study costs to *other* principals under an explicit shared-infrastructure condition.
The attention contribution owns the complete fallback/alert-budget/source comparison.

**Adaptive Deployment of Untrusted LLMs**, arXiv2411.17693v1, primary abstract,
introduction, problem/metric passages and AppendixE.1/usage context examined in
attention/captures/adaptive.txt. It already addresses noncatastrophic distributed
threats, stateful adaptive choices and false-alarm cost of immediate shutdown.
“Adapt the response over time” is not a new idea. Its specific global model-type
and usefulness definition must be distinguished from a finite mixed-principal
capacity setting. No reported effect size was reproduced here.

**Finding Needles in a Moving Haystack**, AAAI2020, retained primary
attention/captures/needles.pdf/.txt: budget-constrained alert prioritization,
strategic adversary and benign users are already explicit. Its loss from undetected
attacks differs from measuring honest clients' service lost because a defensive
restriction succeeded. This observation motivates a particular extension; it does
not establish that the entire IDS/availability literature lacks such metrics.
Targeted search coverage and nearest-work limits remain with the contribution.

**Mediated MARL**, retained arXiv2306.08419v1: Agent0 reread §§2.2–3.2, the
three-player public-good derivation, AppendixA.1 and Tables9–11. Commitment for
k steps is actual action delegation, not a promise. Individual IC/encouragement
constraints average over coalition distributions and values over the commitment
window. The source explicitly leaves strong/group-deviation robustness open.
Its two-stage asymmetric game gives CC payoffs (-1,4) then (2,2), versus DD(0,0)
at both stages; reported discount is .99. A possible new stop between stages
changes realized returns. That is an altered task contract, not a contradiction
of the original uninterruptible result or evidence the learned policies were run
under stops. The authority contribution owns the proposed interruption extension,
its participation calculation and comparison with safe-interruptibility work.

**MAVIC**, arXiv2605.12655v4, primary abstract/introduction, shared-reward model,
method/evaluation passages and Table4 context inspected from authority/captures.
It already addresses instruction changes and interrupted macro-actions, corrects
Bellman continuation, and reports online outcome/completion as well as compliance.
A new proposal cannot claim discovery that interruption affects rewards or that
all prior work ignores realized outcomes. Distinct-principal participation and
budget-balanced remedy are a separate proposed subject from its cooperative
shared-reward problem. Its convergence conditions are not transferred to our model.

## Third lead examined and not promoted: coalition-robust mediation

The direct ancestor **Monderer and Tennenholtz, Strong Mediated Equilibrium** was
retrieved from AAAI's 2006 proceedings (pp.691–696), not the inaccessible 2009
journal body. Crossref/OpenAlex metadata identify the journal DOI
10.1016/j.artint.2008.10.005 and the earlier conference source. The publisher
journal route returned403; the author-hosted full text timed out. Those failures
are access observations. The actual examined primary is
`primary-sources/strong-mediated-aaai.pdf` (SHA-256
`48201238869655c7bce31006aed63d936fc06dd844c46bba932e2aad36412222`).
Read introduction, definitions of correlated/group deviations and mediation,
minimal-mediator lemma, Theorem1 example and existence discussion. Most full
proofs are omitted from this conference paper; no complete proof validation is
claimed.

The source already studies strong group deviations, shows why correlating messages
and actions differs from simple c-acceptability, and constructs an explicit
“Trojan horse” example where one coalition member appears to cooperate while the
other deviates. It characterizes existence only under specified game conditions;
coalition-proof equilibrium is itself a different concept discussed in a footnote.
The 2023 MARL paper explicitly acknowledges this stronger target. Therefore a
new proposal merely showing that unilateral IC does not imply coalition stability
would be old theory, even if relabeled agent oversight. Auditing learned approximate
mediators could still be a useful future extension, but requires actual policy
access/training and a sharper contribution than the already stated open direction.
No existing learned checkpoint or new discriminating result was qualified here.
This lead is not promoted as a third serious alternative just to fill a list.
