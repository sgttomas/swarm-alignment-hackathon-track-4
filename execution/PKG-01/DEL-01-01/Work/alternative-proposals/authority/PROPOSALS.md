## Recommendation

Advance **G — When an authorized safety stop breaks the participation basis of a multi-principal commitment** as a small **analytical assumption-relaxation and compatibility study**, subject to the separate examination already assigned by Agent 0. Its minimum useful result would be a source-matched witness, explicit conditions under which a stop preserves each principal's participation constraint, and limits of proposed repairs. It would not establish deployed prevalence, a new learning algorithm, a general theory of compensation, or superior empirical performance.

The concrete question is: **when a mediator obtains cooperation by promising later benefits in exchange for an early sacrifice, what can an overseer truthfully promise if another principal retains a legitimate right to stop the later stage?** Preserving that stop right, preserving a learner's uninterruptible-task value estimate, and preserving a counterparty's actual outcome are different requirements. Their relation can be examined in an existing two-step game without new models, a general governance platform, or invented field data.

The prospective discriminator is the **joint feasibility of participation, settlement, authority and permitted trigger deviations**, under an explicit contract menu. The shorter commitment comparison exposes an allocation disadvantage that already exists without interruption; stopping did not create that menu gap. Stopping can separately destroy participation relative to abstention in the fixed longer contract. A cancellation-only transfer can repair the latter while failing against a selectable shorter contract, or can match the shorter contract while rewarding a fabricated stop. An agreed upfront allocation behaves differently. This changes a specific contract-selection decision and prevents a misleading claim that “compensation restores cooperation.” The reviewer-derived adverse case below makes this prospective examination concrete without establishing full incentive compatibility.

The generic approval-composition seed is **not promoted**. Current A2A, rich OAuth authorization, CaMeL, and SafeFlow already require or can represent much of its proposed distinction. A separate cancellation-conformance proposal also has close antecedents in Sagas and current A2A discussions. Those sources qualify G's practical interpretation; they do not make an ordinary scope-binding or rollback pattern novel.

## G: bounded case brief

### Problem, recipient, principals, and interaction

The recipient is an operator deciding which commitment/stop arrangement to offer to independently represented principals, and what an oversight dashboard may claim when it reports successful interruption. There are two principals `P0`, `P1`, their task agents, an action-enforcing mediator, and an authorized stop monitor. The agents have different payoffs; the mediator acts for their coalition only while their contract permits it. This is not one common-reward team relabeled as several principals.

Use the published two-step asymmetric Prisoner's Dilemma as the analytical anchor. Interpret it only as a stylized service exchange: `P0` incurs an early cost while `P1` receives early value, and successful later cooperation benefits both. The numerical utilities are the paper's constructed game, not measurements of a real market. A later transfer treatment additionally assumes transferable, enforceable credits; the source's payoff matrix itself does **not** provide a payment API or transferable cash.

The original `k=2` contract prevents withdrawal during its window. In the proposed extension, both principals initially acknowledge a stop right for a trusted monitor: after state 0, an external safety signal may prohibit state 1. This can be a supplier safety interlock or another externally determined constraint on the second-stage operation. The signal schedule is exogenous and independent of whether state 0 used cooperation, so the **same signal and stop policy** apply to all comparisons. A signal discovered only because the first stage ran would be a different selection-dependent model and must be analyzed separately.

The monitor may stop future execution. It has no default authority to confiscate `P1`'s legitimately received early benefit, alter balances, force the prohibited second stage, or certify that another principal is whole. A compensation treatment requires a distinct ex-ante grant, specified funding, and an actual enforceable settlement operation. Without that grant, retaining the early benefit may comply fully with the contract even if `P0` loses. This is not an accusation of breach or deception.

`P0`'s proposed admission policy is to accept a protocol only when its **actual expected discounted return**, under the disclosed stop process and available alternative, is at least that alternative's return. A stronger no-loss-on-every-stopped-path policy is a separate optional criterion. `P1` retains the safety stop: financial disadvantage never becomes permission to continue a forbidden action. The evaluator must report both principals' outcomes, not only coalition sum or stop compliance.

### Question, proposition, and competing explanations

Question: **Which combinations of commitment window, authorized stop rule, outside option, and settlement rights allow a legitimate stop without destroying the agreed participation basis?**

Proposed narrow proposition: a full-window participation calculation from an uninterrupted mediated game does not imply participation viability under an interrupted execution contract. Even when a stop-contingent transfer repairs that implication relative to abstention, it need not repair it relative to an available shorter-window protocol. Proposed repairs must be evaluated under their actual authority, payment timing, funding and deviation possibilities.

Alternatives worth preserving:

- There is no problem for the selected deployment because principals consent to the actual interruption risk and knowingly prefer the arrangement.
- The early sacrifice is reversible at no counterparty cost, so a real authorized rollback suffices.
- The baseline makes later participation available without the sacrifice; the appropriate action is to choose shorter commitment or renegotiate upfront terms, not compensate only after stopping.
- A stop is rare enough that the original arrangement remains individually acceptable relative to abstention, despite some stopped episodes losing value.
- A higher-performing cancellation/settlement mechanism exists already; the useful result is a faithful qualification/replication rather than a new control design.
- A purported safety signal is wrong or strategically generated; the fixed-probability calculation no longer applies.
- The apparent participation problem is an evaluator error: it uses the wrong outside option, treats nontransferable utility as spendable funds, or counts a promised transfer as settled.

### Source-matched formation walkthrough, not a new experiment

Appendix A.1/Table 9 gives mutual cooperation payoffs `(-1, 4)` in state 0 and `(2, 2)` in state 1; mutual defection gives `(0, 0)` in both. Appendix B/Table 11 sets `γ = 0.99`. The two-step source is sequential but the state-1 game is not conditioned on cooperation at state 0. Source text and the parent integration owner's separate reading agree on these entries.

Let `p` be the probability of the authorized stop immediately after state 0. A stop prevents state 1 and adds no other payoff in the initial extension. Let a stop-contingent transfer `t` be settled immediately at this boundary and accounted at state-0 discount time. This explicit timing convention avoids silently discounting one arm differently; a delayed state-1 settlement would contribute `γ p t` instead of `p t`.

| Arrangement / available choice | `P0` expected return | `P1` expected return | What the expression does and does not show |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Both decline; mutual defection throughout | `0` | `0` | A restricted outside option under the same stop rule. Source `k=2` implementation does not allow a noncommitter to join midway. |
| `k=2`, both cooperate, no settlement | `-1 + 2γ(1-p)` | `4 + 2γ(1-p)` | Value of the specified compliant profile, not a full equilibrium derivation. |
| `k=2`, authorized stop-contingent transfer `t` from `P1` to `P0` | `-1 + 2γ(1-p) + p t` | `4 + 2γ(1-p) - p t` | Budget-balanced if transfer actually occurs; no authority or liquidity follows from these utilities. |
| `k=1`, defect in state 0, jointly commit/cooperate in state 1 when allowed | `2γ(1-p)` | `2γ(1-p)` | A protocol-selection alternative supplied by the source; assumes it is available to these principals. |
| `k=2`, agreed immediate upfront transfer `1` when state-0 cooperation executes | `2γ(1-p)` | `3 + 2γ(1-p)` | Changes the first-stage allocation to `(0,3)`; only available with a new agreement and an atomic/otherwise guaranteed transfer. |

Against abstention, the uncompensated `P0` profile is nonnegative iff `p ≤ 1 − 1/(2γ)`, about `0.49495` at the published discount. At `p=0` the source-like profile gives `(0.98, 5.98)`; at `p=1` it gives `(-1,4)`. The cutoff is derived from the toy payoffs, not an empirical estimate or a benchmark success target. If immediate stop-contingent `t=1` is authorized, `P0`'s return is `(1-p)(2γ−1)`, nonnegative for all `p` in this game. This supports only the specified participation comparison; a negative stopped episode without a no-loss clause need not be a policy violation.

**The consequential comparator:** if the operator offers `k=1` and both parties may actually select or recontract into it before entering the interaction, it supplies a protocol-selection alternative. It is **not** a unilateral deviation automatically available inside the fixed `k=2` game. Then `P0`'s advantage from compensated `k=2` over the agreed `k=1` option is `-1 + p t`. Thus `t=1` is still worse for every `p<1`. At `p=0`, no payment triggered only by stopping can close this gap. The upfront transfer `1` preserves `P0`'s `k=1` value while giving `P1` the first-stage surplus `3`, but it changes contract terms and distributes surplus differently. Neither side is obliged to accept it. The broader study should identify conditions for agreement, not declare a universally preferred distribution or retroactively call the source's fixed-contract equilibrium irrational.

Without settlement, the menu gap is exactly `-1` for **every** `p`, including `p=0`: the first-stage sacrifice does not cause the second-stage benefit in this source game. This is a preexisting allocation/menu issue, not evidence that interruption creates a new preference for `k=1`. The fixed-`k=2` crossing below abstention, by contrast, does arise when stopping removes the promised continuation. Keeping these mechanisms separate is the disposition of finding G1 in the [separate examination](../REVIEW.md).

For `p>0`, compensating enough to match `k=1` needs `t≥1/p`. If the only available settlement balance is a separately established `4` credits, this is infeasible below `p=1/4` without other funding. This is a **conditional illustrative boundary**: source utility `4` is not evidence of such a balance. A transfer sufficiently large to cover an attractive outside option can also exceed the recipient's foregone continuation value, creating an incentive to manufacture a stop if the recipient controls the signal. The simple exogenous-`p` comparison would then be invalid. These are reasons to compare upfront terms and refusal, not reasons to weaken the stop right.

### Reviewer-derived strategic witness and author backcheck

The separate examiner independently supplied the following explicit variant and arithmetic in [REVIEW.md](../REVIEW.md), under “G: source-faithful ordinary case and strategic adverse case.” This author re-derived it from the specified branch payoffs and accepts it as a **constructed formation witness**, not a source result, empirical observation or complete equilibrium analysis.

Add one permission absent from the base model: on a no-hazard path, `P0` can costlessly fabricate a signal that forces a stop and receives the same transfer as a genuine stop. The supervisor cannot distinguish the two. For `0<p<1`, both genuine-stop and no-hazard paths have positive probability. Conditional on a no-hazard path after the first stage, continuing gives `P0` discounted future value `2γ=1.98`; fabricated stopping gives immediate `t`. The sunk first-stage cost cancels. Avoiding a **strictly profitable fabricated-stop deviation** therefore requires `t≤2γ`. Matching the mutually selectable `k=1` option requires `p t≥1`. Their conjunction is

`1/p ≤ t ≤ 2γ`, hence `p ≥ 1/(2γ) ≈ 0.505051`, for `0<p<1`.

This is necessary and sufficient for **these two scalar conditions alone**, before funding, `P1`'s participation, consent, detection costs and other deviations. Equality makes `P0` indifferent; it does not strictly deter fabrication. At `p=0`, no finite stop-only transfer repairs the menu gap. At `p=1`, there is no no-hazard path, so this particular fabrication constraint is vacuous. The cutoff is specific to the constructed payoffs and timing, not an estimated safety threshold.

At `p=.25`, `t=4`, compensated `k=2` gives `(1.485,4.485)`, while mutually selected `k=1` gives `(1.485,1.485)`. Thus both profile comparisons can appear acceptable. Yet on a no-hazard path, `P0` gains `4−1.98=2.02` by inducing a false stop. At `p=.75`, `t=4/3` matches `P0`'s menu value without this particular profitable fabrication. Neither example establishes a funded agreement or rules out other deviations. The upfront atomic transfer `1` does not vary with stopping; after it is settled, `P0` still forgoes `1.98` by fabricating a stop. This removes this particular transfer-induced incentive, not every strategic or authority problem.

**Suppression is a different necessary check.** Agent 0 additionally raised concealment of genuine stop evidence. If `P0` can also suppress a genuine signal and thereby obtain the same continuation payoff, with no additional hazard cost or penalty in its payoff, it prefers concealment whenever `t<2γ`. Avoiding both this suppression and the fabrication deviation would force `t=2γ`, yielding indifference on both branches, not robust truthful reporting. This limited observation still leaves `P1`: ignoring any additional hazard cost or penalty, it receives `2γ` by continuing instead of paying `t` on stopping, so it too can have a concealment incentive if it controls evidence. A model of actual hazard losses, reporting costs, sanctions or constrained actions must state those terms rather than assume them away. No complete incentive-robustness claim is warranted from the fabricated-stop bound.

The base model's externally authenticated, mechanically enforced stop has neither an actor-forgeable signal nor an actor-controlled suppression path. A signed actor report alone does not establish that stronger boundary. The strategic variants deliberately change which signals/actions actors control; they must not be combined with the base exogenous-`p` calculation as though the permission set were unchanged. Whatever the information/control design, a financial shortfall does not authorize bypassing a required stop.

For general first payoff `(-c,b)`, continuation `(r0,r1)`, and an immediate stop-contingent transfer, `P0` gets `-c + γ(1-p)r0 + p t`. A chosen outside value `O0` requires `p t ≥ O0 + c − γ(1-p)r0`. A real mechanism must additionally satisfy `P1`'s chosen outside-value constraint, its fund limit, settlement authority, and the permitted-deviation conditions. The conjunction can be empty. This algebra is an initial feasibility witness; the investigation must check the full condition set rather than present this one inequality as complete incentive compatibility.

### Units, views, and reference construction

Unit: one specified two-stage interaction contract and its interrupted/non-interrupted execution paths. The scientific route is finite argument and assumption sensitivity, not sampled model behavior. An episode-level payment/stop trace would be a constructed witness, never evidence of natural frequency.

Agents know their principal's contract, their own utility interpretation, commitment state, and the public stop instruction. In the base analytical case, `p` and the stop rule are common knowledge. The mediator sees the current coalition and can enforce the agreed actions until stopped. The monitor receives the external safety signal directly and mechanically enforces the named stop boundary; agents cannot fabricate or suppress that signal in the base case. Actor-controlled reporting is a separate strategic variant with a different permission set. A settlement component sees the independently granted transfer terms, funding and the settlement record. Credentials, message acknowledgements and completion claims are not substitutes for that record.

The evaluator has the full game, common signal schedule, actual execution/settlement state, and each comparator's available actions. In an imperfect-monitor extension, the evaluator also knows the latent valid-stop condition while the monitor receives only its noisy or incomplete signal. That hidden reference must not be exposed to the monitor and then credited as detection. No ground-truth probability, utility, or consent should be inferred from a public conversation trace alone.

Learning-value estimators are not necessary to answer the finite question. A later illustration may show that a counterfactual uninterrupted value and an actual interrupted return can both be correctly computed yet serve different decisions. It must not relabel the source algorithms as defective for learning their stated objective. No claim that MAVIC or the 2017 pruning algorithm converges on this mixed-interest extension is presently supported.

### Discriminating cases and controls

| Case | Useful test / consequence |
|---|---|
| No stop, source-like enforced `k=2` | Recover the source profile values at `γ=.99`; preserve beneficial ordinary cooperation. |
| Stop before either first-stage action | No sunk contribution; do not manufacture a compensation entitlement. |
| Genuine stop after first stage, no transfer authority | Stop correctly; record each party's return and available recourse as unresolved. Do not infer permission to transfer or claw back. |
| Genuine stop after first stage, pre-funded authorized settlement succeeds | Check actual balances and timing, then compare with both abstention and selectable `k=1`. |
| Transfer promised but unfunded, revoked, failed, or paid later | Preserve the stop and qualify the repair; a correct message or task status cannot establish payment. Recompute discount/feasibility if timing changes. |
| `k=1` unavailable versus available | Distinguish a within-fixed-protocol participation result from a protocol-selection claim. Reuse the same signal schedule. |
| Upfront fair terms versus stop-contingent compensation | Preserve legitimate continuation and compare both principals' utilities, available budget and consent burden. |
| False positive or missing stop evidence | Separate detector correctness from cancellation consequences. Unknown signal quality gives a conditional result, not an estimated deployment benefit. |
| `P0` can fabricate a no-hazard stop | For `0<p<1`, check `t≤2γ` alongside the menu constraint `pt≥1`; preserve endpoint and indifference qualifications. The `p=.25,t=4` witness matches the menu but rewards fabrication. |
| Either principal can suppress genuine stop evidence | Specify which actor can suppress, consequences of unsafe continuation, and enforcement. The fabrication-only bound does not establish truthfulness or complete incentive robustness. |
| Real first-stage reversibility / alternative support for continuation | Admit that an existing rollback or legitimate unaffected action may dominate the proposed settlement. |
| Nontransferable early utility or restricted assets | Reject the transfer treatment rather than silently convert utility into currency. |

The monitor must never obtain a high “safety” score by blocking all cooperation, suppressing the stop right, or counting only total coalition welfare. Legitimate tasks preserved, first-principal shortfall, second-principal cost, stop compliance, settlement success, and evidence/authority assumptions are separate outputs. In a finite analytical study these can be exact quantities; no invented rate or confidence interval is required.

## Seeds examined but not promoted

**Genuine approvals composed into a joint unauthorized action.** A concrete fixture would have two procurement principals approve distinct quotes for distinct beneficiaries, and a broker combine the genuine approvals into a different joint order. Its release decision would need to bind issuer, beneficiary, exact order, conditions and revocation. But RFC 9396 already supplies structured payment authorization including amount and creditor, A2A 1.0 §7.6.4 explicitly prohibits assuming task-state transitions or earlier credentials authorize later messages, CaMeL permits arbitrary security policies over action/arguments/state, and SafeFlow's release rule requires benign-task necessity and target authorization. An underspecified Boolean-approval baseline would manufacture a weak opponent. No inspected real implementation or retained trace establishes the missing binding. A narrowly useful interoperability/conformance fixture remains possible, but the present search does not justify it as a favored scientific alternative.

**Canceling an A2A task while a counterparty has already relied on a completed leg.** The paired traces are easy to specify, but Sagas already distinguishes compensation from restoring prior state and explicitly recognizes observers of partial effects. A2A cancellation does not promise economic undoing; its current public issue discussion also separates prospective suspension, in-flight work and reconciliation. No affected deployed implementation was examined. The case would therefore need a consequential integration claim or genuine observation before becoming more than a known-contract illustration. G uses these distinctions to state its scope rather than claiming a protocol flaw.
